Curtains & drapes: softness, drama, and getting the drop right.

Nothing changes the feel of a room like fabric falling from ceiling to floor. Curtains add warmth, height and a finished look no hard blind can match — if they’re made and hung properly. Here’s what actually matters.

7 min readUpdated Aug 2026Montreal, QC

Curtains vs drapes — is there a difference?

Loosely: curtains are lighter, often unlined; drapes are heavier, lined, more formal. In practice we make both to measure and the choice is really about fabric weight, lining and function. Many rooms use a sheer curtain for daytime with a lined drape over it for evenings — the layered look you see in well-designed homes.

The decisions that matter

1. Fabric & opacity

  • Sheer / voile — soft light, daytime privacy, airy feel.
  • Linen & linen-look — relaxed, textured, very current.
  • Velvet & heavy weaves — luxury, warmth, sound absorption.
  • Blackout-lined — bedrooms and media rooms.

2. Lining

Lining is what separates a curtain that looks good from one that looks expensive. It adds body so the fabric hangs in clean folds, protects the face fabric from sun fade, and — with thermal or blackout lining — adds insulation and darkness.

3. Fullness

Fullness is how much fabric you use versus the width of the window. Skimpy curtains are the most common mistake. We use roughly 2× to 2.5× the track width so the curtains look generous both open and closed.

4. Heading (pleat) style

Wave / S-fold — soft, even ripples; the modern favourite on a track. Pinch pleat — tailored, classic. Eyelet / grommet — casual, on a rod. Pencil pleat — traditional and economical.

5. Track vs rod, and mounting height

A ceiling-mounted track gives the cleanest, tallest look and glides silently — our usual recommendation. Mount high and wide: hanging curtains just above the window frame and extending past its sides makes both the window and the room look bigger.

6. The drop (length)

Kiss the floor (about 1 cm above) for a crisp, tailored finish. Break (2–3 cm on the floor) for a relaxed, luxurious look. Puddle for full drama. Never halfway up the wall. Getting this exact is why we measure every window ourselves.

Honest pros and cons

Pros

  • Unmatched softness, warmth and drama — transforms the feel of a room.
  • Makes ceilings look taller and windows bigger when hung high and wide.
  • Excellent insulation and sound absorption when lined.
  • Endless fabric, colour and pattern choices.
  • Layers beautifully with sheers or blinds.
  • Motorized tracks available.

Cons

  • Take up wall space when open (the stack) — allow for it.
  • Not ideal near water or cooking — kitchens and bathrooms suit blinds better.
  • Dust and need periodic cleaning; check the fabric’s care.
  • Cost more than a basic roller blind because of the fabric quantity and lining.
  • Details are unforgiving — bad measurements show. (We measure for you.)

Who they’re perfect for

If you want the room to feel warm, finished and a little luxurious, curtains are the answer. They’re our first recommendation for living rooms and bedrooms, for tall or feature windows, and for anyone who finds hard blinds a bit clinical. Layer a sheer under a lined drape and you get soft daylight by day and cosy privacy by night.

The layered look

Our most-requested combination: a sheer wave curtain on a ceiling track for the daytime glow, with a lined drape in front for evenings — or a honeycomb blind behind for insulation. Best of both, and it photographs beautifully.

When to choose something else

  • Kitchen, bathroom or a window right by the sink? A wipe-clean roller blind.
  • Tight on wall space, or want a tailored fold instead of a drape? Roman shades.
  • Need insulation without the fabric volume? Honeycomb blinds.

Frequently asked questions

How high should curtains be hung?

As high as you can — ideally from the ceiling or just below it, and extending 15–25 cm past each side of the window. It makes the window and room look bigger.

Should curtains touch the floor?

Yes, or nearly. ‘Kiss’ the floor (about 1 cm above) for tailored, or ‘break’ 2–3 cm onto it for a relaxed, luxe look. Curtains ending mid-wall look unfinished.

What is fullness?

The ratio of fabric width to track width. We use around 2× to 2.5× so curtains look generous. Skimpy curtains are the number-one mistake.

Are curtains good for insulation?

Lined curtains — especially thermal or blackout-lined — insulate very well and also absorb sound. For the best insulation of all, combine with a honeycomb blind.

Do you make and install them?

Yes — every curtain is made to measure for your window and installed free across Montreal, tracks and all.
