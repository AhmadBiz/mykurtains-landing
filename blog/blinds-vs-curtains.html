Blinds vs curtains: which is right for your room?

It’s the first question almost everyone asks us — and the honest answer is “it depends on the room.” Here’s the plain comparison, then a room-by-room verdict, then the trick designers use: both.

6 min readUpdated Aug 2026Montreal, QC

The quick comparison

Blinds
LookClean, tailored, minimal. Vanish when raised.
Light controlPrecise — from sheer to blackout; zebra and slats adjust by degrees.
InsulationHoneycomb: excellent. Roller/zebra: modest.
CleaningEasy — dust or wipe.
Wet roomsYes — easy-clean fabrics.
CostGenerally lower per window.
Curtains
LookSoft, warm, dramatic. Make ceilings feel taller.
Light controlOpen or closed; sheers for filtering, blackout lining for dark.
InsulationVery good when lined; also absorb sound.
CleaningPeriodic; depends on fabric.
Wet roomsNot ideal.
CostMore fabric + lining, so usually higher.

Room by room

Living room

Both. A sheer curtain or zebra blind for daytime light and privacy, with curtains for warmth and evenings. If you must pick one: curtains for a soft, finished room; a zebra blind for a modern one with big glass.

Bedroom

Blackout is the priority — a blackout blind with side channels does the job best, and blackout-lined curtains over it give the hotel look. Cold room? Blackout honeycomb.

Kitchen & bathroom

Blinds. An easy-clean roller or a washable-fabric Roman shade. Curtains and steam/splashes don’t mix. Bathroom with a glass shower? Consider smart film.

Home office

Blinds — glare control matters most on a screen. A zebra or dim-out roller; honeycomb if the window is cold.

Dining room

Curtains or Roman shades — softness and a bit of occasion.

Kids’ room / nursery

Cordless blackout blind — safety and sleep. See the nursery guide.

Patio door / very wide window

Curtains on a track or a wide roller/zebra blind. Roman shades get heavy over ~2 m.

The designer answer: layer them

Walk through any well-designed home and you’ll notice most windows have two treatments: a functional layer for light and privacy (a blind or sheer) and a decorative layer for softness (curtains). You get precise control by day and warmth by night — and it photographs beautifully. It doesn’t have to be expensive: a simple roller or honeycomb behind a pair of lined curtains is a classic.

Our honest rule of thumb

Wet or high-use room → blind. Room you relax in → curtains (or both). Bedroom → blackout first, style second. Cold window → honeycomb, whatever else you add.

Frequently asked questions

Are blinds or curtains better for insulation?

Lined curtains insulate well; honeycomb (cellular) blinds insulate best of any single treatment. Combining a honeycomb blind with lined curtains is the warmest option of all.

Are blinds or curtains cheaper?

Blinds are usually cheaper per window; curtains use more fabric plus lining. Layering both costs more but often replaces the need for a premium single treatment.

Can I have both blinds and curtains on one window?

Yes — it’s the classic designer approach. A blind or sheer for function, curtains for softness. We do this combination constantly.

What’s best for a bedroom?

Blackout. A blackout blind with side channels for real darkness, optionally with blackout-lined curtains over it. Add honeycomb if the room is cold.
